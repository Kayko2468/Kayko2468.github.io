How has the quality of minecraft has degraded over time?

What is Minecraft?

Minecraft is to this day an extremely popular game, having over 425 million copies worldwide and being amongst the three most sold games of all time, only beaten by Tetris with an estimated 520 million copies world-wide. But how has minecraft changed, and has the quality of more recent changes and updates to the game declined as much as many say?

Minecraft is a sandboxed based creativity video-game for home computers and mobile devices that inspires creativity, logic-based thinking, and patience-based recourse gathering. Minecraft has been an internet staple since before 2012, and has since changed dramatically.
However, in the newer years since the game has been claimed by microsoft and is no longer owned by it's original creator, Notch, many of these changes have gotten controversy, many claiming them to be unfitting for the game and unlike it's own nature.

To assess what these supposed problems could be that makes them seem so unfitting, we first have to assess what made the original days of minecraft so appealing to people world-wide.

Visuals and aesthetic

Minecraft is a heavily high-contract and vibrantly colored game, consisting of strong coloring, very basic shadows, minimal texturing details, and basic blockly character and npc models.
This not only makes the game feel more whimsical and toy-like, it also makes it far more visually appealing, especially to young children who are naturally drawn to higher-contrasted color pallets. Additionally, the simplicity makes the game very quick and easy to process visually for all ages, making it a far more relaxing experience.

Similarly, older versions of Minecraft had many dangers pop-out, such as their enemies and Lava, both of which sticking out greatly to most environments, making them immediately noticable and easy to avoid. Alongside these visuals, both enemies and Lava emit noise, making it easy to tell when a danger is nearby.

Music

Minecraft is notoriously known for it's background music, and it's original two albums of music were known for being very soothing and high-quality, however due to disputes between the original music artist and Microsoft when Microsoft had bought out Minecraft, the original artist no longer creates music for the game.

The original artist, known as C418, when still working under Notch hadn't officially worked for him, and instead the exchange between music in the game were treated more as free right to use, however this meant C418 maintained full ownership of their music. However when Microsoft claimed Minecraft, they wanted full ownership over the music, and ultimately they could not reach a compromise with C418.

Creativity and limitations

Throughout Minecraft's peak popularity in 2013, Minecraft had gone between 101 to 119 total blocks, many of which being brick, stair, and slab variations of other blocks. This is a far cry from the count of blocks Minecraft has today, however the limited recourses caused people to be significantly more creative with the tools at their hands to create grandly impressive and visually appealing builds and creations using high ingenuity.

Why Minecraft doesn't feel the same

Minecraft has changed a lot, but it's possible that it's ended up sabotaging its own attractive features.
Minecraft's most appealing factors have since been to some degree depracated. Whilst it's addictive nature tied in with it's vibrant and popping colors, in modern days Minecraft has full shading, darker color schemes, higher and more subtle details in its texturing and imagery, and higher concentrations of audio and visuals to process. Furthermore, Minecraft's appealing music has lost appeal since C418 stopped working for the game, and whilst the current music composers still have high quality additions to the games, in the eyes of some potentially better background tracks than the original albums, they fail to fully capture the style of the originals, some of which not even trying to do so. This leads the music to not flow as well with the game for those who spent years playing the game in it's older states and grew accustomed to the track style.

Lastly, whilst minecraft at the height of its popularity had 120 blocks and 36 enemies and animals, it as of 2026 has a total of about 1,100 total blocks, and 87 total enemies animals. Whilst this gives a far broader range for detail and creatitity, it significantly decreases the need for ingenuity and creative solutions to fill in for the gaps in the styles and palettes of block types that the game once had, as it's a common conception that the human brain is far better at being creative with less recourses, and generally has trouble creating ideas with significantly larger materials to work with, as the range of what they could do blocks out their ability to narrow their perception down to one specific idea, instead getting overwhelmed with the selection.
